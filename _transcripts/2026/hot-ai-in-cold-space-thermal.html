This is 24-7 solar energy above atmosphere and deep-space radiative cooling in a vacuum with near-absolute zero ambient background temperatures. In theory, this gives us zero operational carbon. When we train distributed large language models using techniques like tensor parallelism, nodes perform barrier synchronizations, and all-reduce operations every few microseconds. To achieve the required sub-10 microsecond latencies using laser links or backplanes, compute nodes must be packed into extreme high-density topologies, often within only two or three meters of one another. 3:17 Both architectures force nodes to operate in extreme physical proximity, setting the stage for severe thermal bottlenecks. That physical compactness leads to the proximity thermal paradox. The very spatial density required for low-latency AI synchronization inevitably traps heat and causes intense thermal crosstalk. Let's look at the underlying thermodynamics. The instant net heat gain of a chip depends on how fast it can dissipate heat. In high-density ODCs, that dissipation is heavily restricted by neighboring nodes. In the monolithic systems, we suffer from thermal fluid crosstalk. Cooled or coolant enters upstream nodes, absorbs heat, and flows downstream. When the fluid reaches the downstream nodes along the pipe, it is heavily preheated. Downstream chips are literally being cooled by warm liquid, limiting their heat dissipation. In proximity swarms, we face thermal-radiative crosstalk.

4:22 In the vacuum, satellites reject heat while deep space radiation governed by the Stefan-Boltzmann law. While satellites on the outer periphery enjoying a clear, unobstructed view, satellites in the core of the swarm suffer geometric occlusion. Their heat has nowhere to go, creating local heat traps in the center of the cluster. So why is localized heat trapping such a problem for AI training? Because distributed training relies on all-or-nothing barrier synchronization. 5:28 Then, this single-throttled straggler halts the entire cluster. Global hardware utilization collapses because thousands of healthy, cold GPUs, sit idle, waiting for one hot chip to finish its micro-batch. But throughput loss is only one part of the story. Subjecting chips to rapid temperature swings and sustained heat causes thermal wear and tear, leading to premature failure. This creates permanent space debris and completely undermines the mission's carbon economics. This brings us to the core conceptual contribution of our position paper, the thermal-aware heterogeneity thesis. Traditional AI load balancing frameworks like standard PyTorch distributed data parallel assume uniform cooling, divide training batches into equal slices, and allocate them evenly across all nodes, ignoring the thermal penalty.

6:37 So, our position is that orbital data centers must reject uniform load sharing. In space, structural cooling variance is unavoidable. Therefore, the paradigm shift we propose is simple but fundamental. We must treat the spatial and temporal cooling variance as a primary schedulable computational resource. To prove this thesis, we designed and implemented thermal load balancing, TLB, an orchestration framework that dynamically shifts computation workload to the coolest units in the cluster. Instead of dividing the global data batch equally, we size the micro-batch assigned to each node proportional to a real-time thermal capability score. Every node receives at least one baseline unit of work to maintain gradient synchronization, and the remaining tokens are sliced asymmetrically, based on architectural cooling capacity. For the monolithic structures, we prioritize upstream nodes. Colder upstream nodes receive larger data slices, while the downstream nodes receiving preheated coolant get lighter workloads.

7:43 For proximity swarms, we prioritize periphery nodes using the effective view factor, outer satellites with unobstructed deep-space radiators do the heavy lifting, while core satellites are assigned smaller micro-batches. By matching token volume to cooling capacity, we hope to equalize step completion times across the cluster and prevent hot nodes from thermal throttling.
